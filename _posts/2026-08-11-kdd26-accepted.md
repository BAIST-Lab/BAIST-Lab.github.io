    近日，实验室两项研究成果 “HURST: Heterogeneity-Adaptive Urban Foundation Models for
    Spatiotemporal Prediction via Self-Partitional Mixture-of-Spatial-Experts” 和
    “RePatch: Learning Entropy-Guided Patch Structures with Quantized
    Representations for Time Series Forecasting” 被 KDD 2026 接收。


    **HURST：面向空间异质性的城市时空基础模型**


    城市不同区域往往具有显著不同的数据分布和时空关联模式，这种空间异质性给统一的城市时空基础模型带来了挑战。针对这一问题，HURST 提出面向空间异质性的自适应建模方法，使模型能够根据不同区域的时空特征自动学习更加合适的表示与预测模式。


    该工作进一步探索了空间专家混合、自适应时空学习与提示调优等机制，为提升城市基础模型在不同区域、任务和场景中的适应能力与泛化能力提供了新的思路。


    **RePatch：面向时间序列预测的自适应 Patch 表示学习**


    现有基于 Patch 的时间序列模型通常采用固定的序列划分方式，难以充分适应不同时间序列中不断变化的局部动态结构。针对这一问题，RePatch 探索根据时间序列自身特征自适应学习 Patch 结构，使模型能够更加灵活地组织和表示不同时间尺度下的动态模式。


    同时，该工作结合结构化的离散表示学习，对时间序列中的可复用模式进行更加紧凑的表达，为提升时间序列模型的表示能力及跨场景迁移能力提供了新的建模思路。

    **HURST: Urban Spatiotemporal Foundation Models for Spatial Heterogeneity**


    Different urban regions often exhibit substantially different data distributions and spatiotemporal relationships, making spatial heterogeneity a major challenge for unified urban spatiotemporal foundation models. To address this issue, HURST introduces a heterogeneity-adaptive modeling approach that enables the model to learn representations and prediction patterns suited to the characteristics of different urban regions.


    The work further explores mechanisms including mixture-of-spatial-experts, adaptive spatiotemporal learning, and prompt tuning, providing a new approach to improving the adaptability and generalization of urban foundation models across different regions, tasks, and scenarios.


    **RePatch: Adaptive Patch Representation Learning for Time Series Forecasting**


    Existing patch-based time series models typically rely on fixed sequence segmentation strategies, which may not fully capture the changing local dynamics of different time series. To address this limitation, RePatch explores adaptive learning of patch structures according to the characteristics of the time series itself, allowing the model to represent dynamic patterns at different temporal scales more flexibly.


    The work also incorporates structured discrete representation learning to obtain compact and reusable representations of temporal patterns, providing a new modeling approach for improving representation learning and cross-scenario transfer in time series forecasting.
